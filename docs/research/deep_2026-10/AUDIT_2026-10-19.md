## 3. New findings

1. **The R7 review misread its own source.** The Post Independent article does attribute the slow
   early-winter bookings partly to a post-drought "snow hangover", and cites an Inntopia study in which
   winters after the five worst snow years finished up (occupancy +2.5%, revenue +4.9%). R7 and S1 now
   treat this as a booking delay, not a level cut.
2. **PriceLabs already prices stay-level opportunity cost.** Its minimum-stay engine (2023) weighs
   guaranteed revenue against neighbouring-night booking probability and check-in costs. The specialist
   edge on stays narrows to owner-net economics for 14-to-18-guest parties.
3. **Recommendation logging largely exists.** `price_recommendations` and `recommendation_outcomes`
   store recommended, final, listed and booked prices; `shadow_daily` is empty; the exploration draw
   and approval delay are missing.
4. **Comp coverage has two causes.** Under the engine's own `comp_anchor` rule, the homes have a
   three-comp anchor on 8%, 0% and 0% of December–March nights; pooling all comps would give 23%. Two
   homes are blocked by thin comp-set membership as well as by the 67.5% scrape failure rate.
5. **Comp availability is partly stored.** Quote-level availability for swept dates is in
   `comp_snapshots`; the full daily calendar is fetched and dropped by `_enrich_min_nights` in
   `src/scrape/__init__.py` (the pack cited the wrong file).
6. **Residual risks in `d3e8b92`** (code, not changed here): the road parser can turn a shoulder
   closure into a full-closure flag; the US-40 milepost window may miss the Empire-side approach; the
   snow curve still raises price 12–18% in big-snow years; Open-Meteo's free tier is non-commercial.
