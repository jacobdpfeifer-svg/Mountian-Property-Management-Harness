# Simulator and repo overhaul — plan (2026-10-14)

Goal: a pricing system that makes property owners the most money. Every change below
is judged by one question: does it make the engine's measured owner profit more
trustworthy, or larger? A simulator that cannot tell a good price from a bad one is
worse than none, because it tunes the engine in the wrong direction.

Source audit: the 2026-10-13 full-run audit (conversation 2026-10-14). Decisions
recorded at the end of this file.

## What changed the picture

The homes' **real** winter rate card (publicly listed prices, operator DB per-home
monthly averages):

| Home | Dec listed (avg) | Feb listed (avg) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cloud 9 | $1,994 | $2,510 |
| Overlook Ridge | $1,732 | $2,327 |
| Summit Haus | $1,881 | $2,393 |

Real December bookings so far closed well above $2,000/night. Booked figures stay in
the local, gitignored export (`data/exports/`): **this GitHub repo is public**, so
confidential revenue data is never committed.

The simulator's whole market sits at $620–$910 (shopper cap = multiplier × $700,
competitors $620–$700, flat $900). The peak caps and base-anchor rates awaiting sign-off
(Summit $793, Overlook $916, Cloud 9 $1,035) are well under half of what the homes actually
list and book at peak. Scraped comps for these homes ask $1,600–$2,300 at peak.

**Consequence:** every simulated revenue number so far was produced in a market about
2–3× too cheap. Relative findings (the floor spiral, the guardrail behaviour) still hold
as mechanisms. The dollar levels, the peak caps and the base rates do not.

## Phase 0 — Revenue safety (now)

1. Do **not** sign off the base rates or peak caps as prices. The base-anchor
   *mechanism* (stop the off-peak floor spiral) can still be approved separately.
2. Add a preflight check that fails when a configured peak cap or base rate is below
   the home's realized peak booked price (from an aggregate rate-card file), so a
   simulator-tuned number cannot silently cut real rates.
3. Guesty writes stay at zero (unchanged).

## Phase 4 — Engine: peak pricing

Today the peak price is the ceiling on 100% of Jan–Mar recommendations, because peak
elasticity −0.65 puts the RevPAN optimum above any ceiling. The fix combines booking
pace with learned elasticity:

| # | Change |
|---|---|
| 4.1 | Peak reference price from comps at matching capacity and the home's own realized prices, not the self-referential ceiling |
| 4.2 | Booking-pace adjustment: on-the-books occupancy at each lead time vs a reference booking curve; ahead of pace → raise, behind → lower, bounded per day |
| 4.3 | Learned elasticity with shrinkage: start from the configured prior and move toward what observed price/booking variation supports, weighted by evidence; clamped |
| 4.4 | Optimize owner net on the all-in price (fee- and LOS-aware), not nightly RevPAN |
| 4.5 | The ceiling becomes a safety cap. Diagnostic: share of peak recs at ceiling should fall well below 100% |
| 4.6 | Booking-probability calibration (L1 Brier is worse than baseline): reliability check and fix |
| 4.7 | Retire hard-coded peak caps; re-derive base-anchor rates from the real rate card |

**Finding — engine policies in the calibrated market (simulated owner net, 5 seeds, normal):**
